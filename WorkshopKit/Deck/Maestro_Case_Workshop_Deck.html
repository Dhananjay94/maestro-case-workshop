The scenario

A motor insurer handles car claims

A customer has an accident and files a claim. The insurer has to decide what to pay, and decide it fairly and fast.

Pay genuine claims fast

Customers judge an insurer by how quickly it pays.

Catch fraud early

A suspicious claim needs a closer look before any money moves.

Stay compliant

Every decision must be recorded and explainable.

And no two claims behave the same way.

The business process

Five steps from claim to closure

Step 1

Intake

Register the claim. Read the documents. Check the policy.

Step 2

Assessment

Estimate the damage. Check the risk of fraud.

Step 3

Review

An adjuster looks at the claim and decides.

Step 4

Settlement

Work out the amount. A senior approver signs off big claims. Pay.

Step 5

Closure

Tell the customer. Update the records.

This is how the business reviews the process on paper.

What we still lack

Good results, but in silos

Each team's automation works, but on its own.

A task fails. Which claim is stuck? Who is affected? Who even knows?

No overall orchestrationNo end-to-end viewNo observability across teams

Orchestration

Maestro BPMN joins them in one flow

Orchestration and one view of the whole process. It works well when the order is known.

But does it fit?

Real claims are dynamic and cyclic

When the process is not strictly defined, a fixed sequence cannot keep up.

Real life

A claim file moves from room to room

In a real office, the file is handed from room to room. Three claims, three stories.

1

Straight through

A rear-end collision at a traffic light. All documents are in, the policy is valid and the fraud risk is low. The claim is paid.

2

A document is missing

A side collision. The repair estimate was forgotten, so the file waits in a side room for the customer. When it arrives, the claim carries on.

3

Suspected fraud

A head-on collision. The documents contradict each other and there are earlier claims. An investigator confirms fraud, and the claim is denied.

Case management

The rooms become stages

Room → Stage
Activity → Task
Claim file → Case
Hand-off decision → Rule

Same picture, now a case: the stages hold the tasks, the rules decide the path.

What is case management

Three tools, used together

RPABPMNCase management
Use it whenOne repetitive taskThe order of steps is knownIt depends on what turns up
Unit of workA taskA process instanceA case, the claim's file
The heroThe botThe sequenceThe case and its rules

A case is one claim’s file. It moves through stages, rules decide the path, and it calls your bots, agents and processes.

The building blocks

What a case is made of

Case

One claim, one record, from start to finish.

Stage

Where the case is now: primary or secondary.

Task

Work in a stage: agent, function, API workflow or RPA bot.

Human task

A person decides, in an app.

Rules

Entry, exit and complete: when to start, leave and finish.

Variables

The shared data that rules read.

SLA

Time limits, with escalation when late.

Case app

See every case. Pause, resume, retry.
